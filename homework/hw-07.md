# Part 1 - Critique of ACME-SEC-POL-014 v2.3

**1. Header (12.1.1, 12.1.2).** The draft cites PCI DSS v3.2.1, which was retired in March 2024, and carries a 2022 effective date with no sign of annual review. Corrected language: "This policy implements PCI DSS v4.0.1 and SOX ITGC. The CISO reviews it at least every 12 months and after any significant change."

**2. Section 2, Scope (6.3.3, 12.5.1, 12.5.2).** Network devices are excluded and deferred to an unnamed runbook, and the scope never mentions the CDE or an asset inventory. Acme has 14 network devices, some at the CDE boundary, and 6.3.3 applies to all system components. Corrected language: "This policy covers every component in or connected to the CDE and every SOX financial-reporting system, including network devices and the patch infrastructure. Scope is confirmed at least every 12 months."

**3. Section 3, Identification (6.3.1, 6.3.2).** Vulnerabilities are found through "word of mouth" and "industry news," with no authoritative sources, no risk-ranking method, and no software inventory. Corrected language: "The SOC Analyst reviews CISA KEV, NVD, and vendor advisories every business day. The GRC Analyst ranks each applicable vulnerability within two business days."

**4. Section 4, Timelines (6.3.3, 6.5.1, 6.5.2).** One monthly window applies to every patch, and failed patches "carry forward" with no limit. There is no emergency path, testing, or back-out plan, so a critical patch can wait about 30 days. Corrected language: "Deadlines run from vendor release: Critical 7 days (72 hours if on KEV), High 14, Medium 30, Low 90. Out-of-window changes are CAB-approved, and every patch has a back-out plan."

**5. Section 5, Scanning (11.3.1, 11.3.1.2, 11.3.1.3, 11.3.2, 11.3.2.1).** Internal scans happen annually and cover servers only. There is no authenticated scanning, no external ASV scanning, and the tool is "of their choosing." Corrected language: "Authenticated internal scans run with Nessus Professional at defined frequencies, and external ASV scans run at least quarterly and after any significant change."

**6. Section 5, Findings (11.3.1, 6.3.3).** Findings are reviewed "as time permits," with no owner, deadline, or rescan. Corrected language: "Every confirmed vulnerability is ticketed with an owner and a due date and is closed only after a Security Team rescan."

**7. Sections 5-6, Roles (11.3.1, 6.1.2, 12.1.3, 12.1.4).** System administrators identify, patch, and verify their own work, there is no CISO or Security Team role, and the owner is just "IT Department." Corrected language: "The CISO owns this policy. The Security Team verifies; System Administration and Network Engineering remediate. No one both remediates and verifies."

**8. Sections 7-8, Reporting and Review (12.1.2, 12.4.1-12.4.2 if Acme is a service provider).** Reporting happens "as needed," the review cadence is left to the IT Director with no minimum, and there are no metrics or evidence retention. Corrected language: "KPIs are reported monthly, evidence is retained seven years, and the policy is reviewed at least every 12 months."

**Missing entirely:** an exception and risk-acceptance process (12.3.1, Appendices B and C), an emergency patching path (6.3.3, 6.5.1), and end-of-life software handling.

# Part 2 - Vulnerability Management Policy (ACME-SEC-POL-014 v3.0)

**Frameworks:** PCI DSS v4.0.1, SOX ITGC. **Owner:** CISO. **Review:** at least every 12 months.

**Assumptions.** The Environment Profile is silent on a few points, so this policy assumes the following. (A1) "System Owner" is the business owner recorded per system in the inventory, for example the Controller for the financial-reporting databases. (A2) WSUS runs on a Windows Server 2022 host that will be recorded in the inventory within 14 days. (A3) Nessus Professional is a single-operator tool with no role-based access. (A4) PostgreSQL 15 comes from the PostgreSQL project repository, since Ubuntu 22.04 ships version 14. (A5) After-hours coverage is escalation only, through a best-effort Security on-call rotation that the CISO confirms.

## 1. Policy Statement and Governance

**Purpose and authority.** Acme will identify, risk-rank, remediate, and independently verify vulnerabilities on every system that handles cardholder data, authenticates access to it, or supports financial reporting. That covers 6 DMZ web servers, 10 app/API servers, 4 PostgreSQL servers, 2 domain controllers, 3 file/print servers, about 200 Windows 11 workstations, 14 network devices, and the patch infrastructure (Ansible, WSUS, Nessus). The CISO owns the policy, and the CISO, IT Director, and CFO approve it. It resolves the three notations in the latest Report on Compliance: no risk-ranking process (6.3.1), no ASV contract (11.3.2), and no segregation of duties between patching and verification (11.3.1).

**If the policy is not followed.** A missed Critical or High deadline without an approved exception is escalated to the CISO the next business day. The CISO may require emergency change handling or isolation of the asset from the CDE. Willful non-compliance, such as closing tickets without remediation, is handled under Acme's disciplinary policy. Unremediated Critical or High findings are PCI failures and, for the four database servers, SOX control deficiencies.

**Roles and responsibilities.** The **CISO** owns and approves the policy, grants Critical and High exceptions, funds and signs the ASV contract, signs the monthly KPI package, and reports quarterly to the CFO. The **Security Engineer** is the sole Nessus operator, runs scans on schedule, confirms findings within two business days, performs every verification rescan, and is the only role that can close a ticket. The **GRC Analyst** owns risk ranking (within two business days), the exception register, SOX evidence, the KPIs, and false-positive approval, and reviews scan scope monthly. The **SOC Analyst** reviews the intelligence sources each business day, opens tickets, handles after-hours KEV escalation, and serves as trained backup scan operator. The four-person **System Administration** team remediates within SLA using Ansible (Linux) and WSUS (Windows), files changes with rollback plans, and marks tickets "Remediated - Pending Verification." It cannot close tickets or run verification scans, and it provisions the scan accounts. The two-person **Network Engineering** team patches the 14 devices under the same SLAs, supplies the vendor list, provides read-only scan access, and applies emergency containment when directed. **System Owners** approve downtime for High and above, co-sign exceptions, and confirm criticality annually. The **CAB** (IT Director as chair, with a Security representative) approves changes per the matrix, maintains pre-approved templates for routine patching, and approves out-of-window or emergency changes.

## 2. CVSS Classification and SLA Matrix

**Two clocks.** The *vendor-disclosure* clock starts when the vendor releases a complete fix. It measures Acme's exposure to a publicly known flaw, which is what PCI 6.3.3's "within one month of release" measures. The *scan-discovery* clock starts when a scan finding is confirmed and measures detection latency. Both exist because Acme scans weekly to monthly, so a flaw can sit undetected for weeks. The earlier deadline governs, and a flaw already past its vendor deadline when found is overdue on the day it is confirmed.

**Risk ranking (6.3.1).** The GRC Analyst starts from the NVD CVSS base score, using the CNA or vendor score if NVD analysis is pending. A vulnerability is upgraded to Critical if it is on CISA KEV or actively exploited, and to at least High if its EPSS probability is 0.50 or higher. It moves up one tier if the asset is internet-exposed and the score is 7.0 or higher. CDE systems, financial-reporting databases, domain controllers, and the patch infrastructure are never ranked below High at a score of 7.0 or higher. A downgrade is allowed only for a documented "Not Applicable" disposition approved by the GRC Analyst.

**SLA by tier.**
- **Critical (CVSS 9.0-10.0, or any KEV or actively exploited vulnerability):** 7 days from vendor disclosure, or 72 hours for KEV, and 5 days from scan discovery (3 for KEV). The fix proceeds as an emergency change approved by the IT Director and a Security representative within four business hours, with the CISO and System Owner informed the same day.
- **High (7.0-8.9):** 14 days from vendor disclosure and 10 days from scan discovery. An expedited CAB approves it, with the Security representative and System Owner signing off, and the IT Director must approve any out-of-window execution.
- **Medium (4.0-6.9):** 30 days from vendor disclosure and 21 days from scan discovery. It proceeds as a pre-approved standard change, with the System Owner notified three business days ahead.
- **Low (0.1-3.9):** 90 days from vendor disclosure and 60 days from scan discovery, handled as a pre-approved standard change in the quarterly cycle.

**Real CVE examples** (scores as shown in NVD-fed records):

**CVE-2025-59287 (WSUS remote code execution, CVSS 9.8)** is Critical on the 72-hour KEV track. Microsoft's complete out-of-band fix arrived on Oct 23, 2025 and CISA listed it on Oct 24, so the deadline is Oct 27, 2025. Because Acme patches Windows through WSUS, a compromised WSUS server reaches every Windows system, including the domain controllers.

**CVE-2025-1094 (PostgreSQL libpq SQL injection, fixed in 15.11, CVSS 8.1)** is High. It affects Acme's four CDE database servers. It was published Feb 13, 2025, so the deadline is Feb 27, and the Feb 22 maintenance window falls inside it. An expedited CAB handles it, with the Controller approving the database restart.

**CVE-2025-23419 (nginx TLS session-resumption client-certificate bypass, CVSS 4.3)** is Medium. It affects the DMZ Nginx servers only if client-certificate authentication is used on a shared default server. The deadline is Mar 7, 2025 via standard change, and if that configuration is not present the finding is dispositioned Not Applicable with evidence attached.

## 3. Identification and Scanning Program

**Sources.** The SOC Analyst reviews these every business day: CISA KEV, NVD, Ubuntu Security Notices, Microsoft MSRC and Patch Tuesday, PostgreSQL and nginx security announcements, network-vendor PSIRT feeds, CISA/US-CERT advisories, and FIRST EPSS. KEV and NVD also generate automated Jira alerts. The Security Team has three people and no 24/7 coverage, so after-hours is escalation only. A KEV match pages the Security on-call, who acknowledges within two hours and requests an emergency change, and Network Engineering can apply containment while the patch is staged.

**Scan frequency.** All frequencies exceed PCI's three-month minimum for internal scans, and a quarterly scan package is filed as evidence. The six DMZ servers and externally reachable device interfaces get weekly credentialed scans plus monthly uncredentialed scans from an attacker's vantage point, and an ASV scan at least quarterly (target monthly) and after any significant change. The CDE application and database servers, CDE-boundary devices, domain controllers, and patch infrastructure get weekly credentialed scans. The three file/print servers and other network devices get monthly scans. Workstations get a monthly credentialed scan plus a verification check ten days after each Patch Tuesday. Every asset is also scanned within five business days of a significant change.

**ASV gap (RoC 11.3.2).** Nessus Professional cannot produce a PCI-valid external scan. The CISO signs an ASV contract within 30 days and the first scan runs within 60 days. Until then, the Security Engineer runs weekly external Nessus scans as a temporary control that is not PCI-valid, expires with the contract, and is never extended past 90 days. A passing ASV report has no vulnerability at CVSS 4.0 or higher.

**Tools and credentials.** Acme uses Nessus Professional for both credentialed and uncredentialed scans, with credentialed scanning as the default because Ubuntu backports fixes and banners can show old version numbers. System Administration provisions the scan accounts (restricted sudoers on Linux, a GPO-scoped non-interactive account on Windows) and they are used only through Nessus. This way the Security Team holds no production admin credentials, and System Administration cannot use Nessus.

**Segregation of duties (RoC 11.3.1).** Whoever remediates never verifies. Sysadmin self-checks do not count as evidence, and sysadmins may not run Nessus. In Jira, the "Verified/Closed" transition is limited to the Security Team and requires rescan evidence. The GRC Analyst independently reviews scan scope and credentials each month.

**Findings, false positives, and rescanning.** A *finding* is raw scanner output and has no SLA. A *confirmed vulnerability* is a finding the Security Engineer has validated within two business days by checking that the asset is in the inventory, that credentialed package or version evidence supports it, and that the vulnerable configuration is actually present. Only the GRC Analyst approves false positives, and each suppression is re-reviewed quarterly. After remediation, the Security Engineer rescans within two business days. A failure reopens the ticket with the original clock still running, and rescans repeat until the finding clears or an approved exception exists.

## 4. Exception and Risk Acceptance

**Who can grant an exception, and for how long.** A **Critical** exception needs the CISO and System Owner, with the IT Director consulted, and lasts at most 30 days, with one renewal of up to 30 days requiring the CISO and CFO (60 total). A **High** exception needs the CISO and System Owner and lasts at most 60 days, with one renewal of up to 30 days requiring the CISO and CFO (90 total). A **Medium** exception needs the GRC Analyst and System Owner (plus the CISO if the asset is in the CDE or a financial database) and lasts at most 90 days with one 90-day renewal. A **Low** exception needs the GRC Analyst and System Owner and lasts at most 180 days with one 180-day renewal. Nobody approves an exception on a system they administer. Any Critical or High exception reaching 90 cumulative days must become a funded remediation project approved by the CISO and CFO, which addresses the QSA's concern that compensating controls are unsustainable.

**Compensating controls.** Critical and High exceptions require controls that cover the same attack path, modeled on PCI Appendices B and C. These include network isolation or port restriction (for example limiting WSUS ports), disabling the vulnerable feature or applying the vendor workaround, daily SOC log review for exploitation indicators, restricted administrative access, and continued weekly credentialed scanning. The request must state how each control reduces the risk and how its operation will be evidenced.

**Tracking and review.** Each exception is a Jira Service Management ticket linked to the vulnerability, with CVE, asset, rating, reason, controls, approvers, and expiry as required fields. The GRC Analyst exports the register monthly with a hash as seven-year SOX evidence. Reminders fire at 30, 14, and 7 days before expiry, and the Security Engineer verifies that the controls are operating every month. An expired exception returns the item to overdue status and escalates it automatically.

**Mandatory CISO escalation.** The CISO is notified of any Critical or High deadline missed without an exception, any KEV match on an Acme asset, any exception request involving a database server, CDE system, domain controller, or patch infrastructure, and any Critical or High renewal. Escalation also covers a failed compensating control, a disputed rating or refused downtime, two failed rescans, an ASV failure or missed quarter, an uninventoried asset or CDE scan gap, and any ticket closed without a Security rescan.

## 5. KPIs (monthly to the CISO and IT Director, quarterly to the CFO)

**SLA compliance by tier** is the number remediated by the governing deadline divided by the number due in the month, times 100. The targets are 100% for Critical, at least 95% for High, 90% for Medium, and 85% for Low. It maps to PCI 6.3.3 and 11.3.1 and to SOX change and operations controls.

**Mean time to remediate** is the sum of (fix date minus vendor release date) divided by the count remediated, by tier. The targets are 5 days or less for Critical (72 hours for KEV), 10 days for High, and 25 days for Medium. It maps to PCI 6.3.3.

**Credentialed scan coverage** is assets scanned with credentials divided by assets in the inventory, times 100. The target is 100% for the CDE and domain controllers and at least 98% overall. It maps to PCI 11.3.1, 11.3.1.2, and 12.5.1 and to SOX scope completeness.

**Independent verification rate** is closed tickets verified by a Security rescan divided by all closed tickets, times 100. The target is 100%. It maps to PCI 11.3.1 and to SOX segregation of duties.

**Exception health** counts open Critical and High exceptions and exceptions past expiry. The targets are no more than two open at any time and zero past expiry. It maps to PCI Appendices B and C and 12.3.1 and to SOX risk-acceptance evidence.

**KEV response** is the share of matching KEV entries triaged within one business day, and the share remediated or mitigated within 72 hours. The target is 100% for both. It maps to PCI 6.3.1 and 6.3.3.

**External scan compliance** is the number of passing ASV quarters in the trailing four. The target is 4 of 4. It maps to PCI 11.3.2.

Reports, scan exports, Jira histories, and exception exports are kept in a write-protected evidence repository for seven years for SOX-relevant systems and at least 12 months for others. An auditor can then trace any database vulnerability from intelligence entry through risk ranking, CAB approval, remediation, rescan, and closure.
